Backtesting Engine Overview
Written: 2026-04-21

PURPOSE

The backtesting engine was built to answer a fundamental question: given the price action data we observe on Polymarket temperature markets, what combination of entry price, number 
of bins, trailing stop, take profit, and exit rules would have produced the most profitable results? Rather than guessing at thresholds or relying solely on the weather model's 
probability estimates, the engine replays actual historical price movements and simulates any trading strategy against real market data.

The motivation came from analyzing 153 closed paper trades and discovering that the weather model's probability estimates were systematically overconfident — when the model said 
30% probability, the bin only won 8% of the time. Meanwhile, the market's prices were better calibrated. The analysis also revealed that 51% of losing trades were once profitable 
before reversing, and that a simple 12% trailing stop (instead of the fixed -30% hard stop) would have turned a -$14,000 loss into a +$13,000 gain on the same set of trades. These
 findings led to the idea of a market-price-driven strategy that doesn't rely on weather model probabilities at all, and the backtesting engine is the tool that will determine the 
 optimal parameters for that strategy.


DATA COLLECTION

Three new database tables were created to support backtesting:

1. bin_price_history — This is the primary price data table. It stores periodic snapshots of the YES price, NO price, volume, and liquidity for every bin in every active event. 
Each row includes the event_id, contract_id, city, date, and a recorded_at timestamp. This table is populated from two sources. First, the trading scan (which runs hourly at :15 
UTC) writes one row per bin for every event it analyzes — this captures all bins, not just the ones selected for trading. Second, and more importantly, the WebSocket price stream 
now subscribes to ALL active event tokens (not just held positions) and a scheduled job writes the current live prices from the WebSocket cache to this table every 5 minutes. This
 gives roughly 288 price snapshots per bin per day, which is granular enough to simulate trailing stops and take-profit exits with reasonable accuracy.

2. event_resolutions — This records which bin won each event after the market resolves. Each row contains the event_id, city, date, the winning contract_id, the winning bin's range
 (range_low and range_high), and timestamps. This table is populated by two mechanisms. When the monitor loop detects that a position has resolved (a market closed and a winner was 
 determined), it records the winning bin if the position was on the winning side. Additionally, a new function called _scan_event_resolutions runs at the end of every monitor cycle 
 and checks all past events (events whose date has passed) for any bin that reached 90%+ in the decision_snapshots — this captures resolutions for events the bot didn't trade, which 
 is critical because the backtesting engine needs to know the winner for every event, not just the ones where we had a position.

3. The existing decision_snapshots table (37,444 rows already) also serves as a price history source. It captures the market_price for every bin at every hourly trading evaluation. 
The backtesting engine will use bin_price_history when available (higher frequency) and fall back to decision_snapshots for older data.

WHAT IS CAPTURED

For every active event on Polymarket, the following data points are now captured every 5 minutes via the WebSocket price snapshot job: the YES price (market probability) for each 
bin, the NO price (1 - YES price), and the contract_id linking it to a specific temperature range. The hourly trading scan additionally captures volume and liquidity data. Combined, 
this means that for an event that exists for 48 hours before resolution, each bin will have approximately 576 price data points (48 hours x 12 snapshots per hour). This is sufficient
 to simulate entry at any price level, trailing stops at any percentage, take-profit exits at any threshold, and cross-bin exit rules with high fidelity.

The WebSocket subscription was expanded from "only tokens for held positions" to "all tokens for all active events." The _ws_loop already refreshed subscriptions every 60 seconds — 
it now calls load_all_event_tokens() alongside load_open_position_tokens() during each refresh. A metadata mapping (_token_metadata) links each token_id back to its event_id, 
contract_id, city, and date so that price updates can be written to bin_price_history with full context.

CONSIDERATIONS BEFORE RUNNING

The backtesting engine requires resolved events with price history to produce meaningful results. As of 2026-04-21, most of the resolved events in the database (42 events from 4/16 
and 4/20) were already at 100% price when data collection began — their price paths start near $1.00, making them useless for simulating entry strategies. The engine will report "no 
trades" for these events because no bin falls within the entry price range (e.g., 25-45 cents). You should wait at least 2-3 days after these changes are deployed before running the
 backtester, so that new events can be captured from discovery through resolution with complete 5-minute price paths. After one week of data collection, you should have 50-100+ 
 resolved events with full price histories, which is enough for statistically meaningful parameter optimization.

The engine uses chronological price paths — it walks through each snapshot in time order, simulating what would have happened if you entered at the first snapshot where the price 
met your criteria. It does not look ahead or use future information. However, it does not account for execution slippage, spread costs, or liquidity impact. Real trades may fill at 
slightly worse prices than the snapshot values. For positions sized at $500 on bins with $500+ liquidity, this impact should be small (1-3 cents typically).

The 5-minute snapshot interval means the engine cannot simulate sub-5-minute price movements. A trailing stop that would have triggered between snapshots will instead trigger at 
the next snapshot. This could slightly overstate or understate the actual exit price. For a more precise simulation, the interval could be reduced to 1-2 minutes, but this would 
significantly increase database size (roughly 500-1000 rows per event per day).


NEW FILES

bot/scripts/strategy_backtest.py — The backtesting engine itself. This is a standalone script that reads from the database, simulates trades, and prints results. It does not modify 
any data or affect live trading. It accepts command-line arguments for individual parameter testing (--min-price, --max-price, --bins, --trail) or the --sweep flag for exhaustive
 parameter search. It defines a StrategyParams dataclass with all configurable parameters: min_price, max_price, max_bins, trail_pct, trail_activation, take_profit, cross_bin_exit,
  hard_stop_pct, bet_size, and use_ratchet. The SimTrade dataclass captures the full lifecycle of each simulated trade including entry/exit prices, times, reasons, P&L, and peak
   price.

bot/scripts/trade_analysis.py — Created earlier in this session, this script analyzes actual closed trades (not simulated) to assess model calibration, feature importance, and 
threshold sensitivity. It includes a calibration analysis (reliability diagram), feature breakdown by bucket, logistic regression for feature importance, threshold simulation grid,
 closing line value analysis, and MFE/MAE analysis. Run with: python scripts/trade_analysis.py


NEW VARIABLES AND CONFIGURATION

No new .env variables were added for the data collection — it runs automatically as part of the existing infrastructure. The price snapshot job is hardcoded to run every 5 minutes 
via the scheduler (CronTrigger with minute="*/5"). If you want to change the frequency, modify the CronTrigger in main.py where the price_snapshot job is defined.

The backtesting engine's parameters are all defined in the StrategyParams dataclass within strategy_backtest.py and are passed via command-line arguments or the sweep function. They
 are not .env variables because they are research parameters, not production configuration. When you identify the optimal parameters through backtesting, those values will be
  implemented as .env variables in the new market_price_value strategy.


THE --SWEEP FUNCTION

Running python scripts/strategy_backtest.py --sweep performs an exhaustive parameter search across 432 combinations. It tests the following dimensions independently first (to show 
the effect of each parameter in isolation), then tests all combinations together:

1. Entry price threshold — Tests minimum entry prices from 15% to 40% in 5% increments, holding other parameters constant at 1 bin and 12% trailing stop. This shows which price
range produces the best results.

2. Number of bins — Tests 1, 2, and 3 bins per event at a fixed 30% entry threshold. This shows whether buying multiple bins (hedging) improves or hurts results.

3. Trailing stop percentage — Tests stops from 5% to 25%, showing the tradeoff between capturing gains (tight stop) and avoiding whipsaws (loose stop).

4. Ratcheting vs flat trail — Compares a fixed 12% trail against an adaptive trail that tightens as price rises (15% below 50c, 12% at 50-65c, 8% at 65-80c, 5% above 80c).

5. Take profit levels — Tests selling at 50%, 60%, 70%, 80%, 90%, or holding to resolution. This determines whether locking in gains early beats waiting for the full $1.00 payout.

6. Cross-bin exit thresholds — For 2-bin portfolios, tests exiting the weaker bin when the stronger bin reaches 60%, 70%, 75%, 80%, 90%, or never. This determines when to cut a 
likely loser based on a sibling bin's strength.

7. Hard stop levels — Tests the fixed stop-loss fallback (before the trailing stop activates) at various levels from 15% to no stop at all.

After the individual tests, the sweep runs all 432 combinations (4 entry prices x 2 bin counts x 3 trail percentages x 3 take-profit levels x 2 cross-bin options x 3 hard-stop 
levels) and ranks the top 10 by total P&L. It prints the winning combination with full details including exit reason breakdown, showing exactly how many trades exited via trailing 
stop vs take profit vs resolution vs hard stop.

The output is designed to be self-explanatory: each row shows the parameter combination, number of trades, win count, win rate, average P&L per trade, total P&L, and maximum 
drawdown. Combinations marked with "<<<" in the individual tests indicate positive average P&L. The exhaustive search at the end identifies the globally optimal combination that may
 not be obvious from the individual parameter tests.
