Trading logic overhaul — what changed and why
The problem we solved
Before this update, the bot was a one-way valve. It entered trades based on edge detection and then did absolutely nothing until the market resolved — every position was held to binary resolution at $1.00 or $0.00. If the forecast shifted dramatically against a position, if the observed temperature already blew past a bin's range making it a guaranteed loser, or if the market moved heavily in our favor offering a chance to lock in profit — the bot just sat there. The monitor loop updated unrealized P&L every hour for display purposes but never acted on it. This meant we were holding guaranteed losers to zero, watching winning positions risk reversal, and ignoring every signal the database was collecting about whether a trade's original thesis still held.

What we built: the exit engine
The core addition is a new module called position_eval.py that runs after every trading cycle. It evaluates every open filled position by comparing its current state to its state at entry, then classifies it into one of five categories.

INVALIDATED means the observed temperature has already exceeded the bin's upper bound by more than a safety buffer (currently 0.5°C, controlled by EXIT_DEAD_BUFFER_C). For example, if we hold a YES position on the 70-72°F bin and the day's observed max has already hit 73.5°F, that bin cannot win. The buffer exists because our observation source (Visual Crossing) may not perfectly match the contract's official resolution source, so we don't declare a position dead on a razor-thin margin. We also require either that the day is materially advanced (past 2 PM local) or that the model itself confirms near-zero probability, preventing premature exits on a hot morning spike that might not hold. These positions are sold immediately at whatever residual market value remains — recovering even $0.02 on a dollar is better than holding to certain $0.00.

DYING means the model's probability for this bin has collapsed since entry. Specifically, the current model probability is below 5% (EXIT_DYING_PROB_THRESHOLD) and the entry probability was above 15% (EXIT_DYING_ENTRY_MIN), with a drop of more than 10 percentage points. This catches cases where the forecast shifted enough to make the bin very unlikely but the observed temperature hasn't technically invalidated it yet. The key design principle here, which came from your feedback, is that the comparison is entry-vs-current (cumulative shift), not just the latest 2-hour forecast delta. A position that deteriorated gradually across four forecast pulls — each one within threshold — would be missed by a latest-delta approach but caught by the cumulative comparison.

WEAKENED covers two scenarios. First, the cumulative forecast shift since entry exceeds a threshold expressed in units of the entry-time σ (controlled by EXIT_WEAKENED_SIGMA_SHIFT, currently 1.0). This is measured as the difference between the current adjusted μ and the entry-time adjusted μ, retrieved from the decision snapshot that was captured when the trade was placed. Critically, this check is side-aware: a shift is only "against" the position if it moves μ away from the bin for a YES position, or toward the bin for a NO position. A shift in the supportive direction doesn't trigger an exit. Second, edge reversal: if the model now believes the bin's probability is below the market price for a YES position (or above it for NO), the original edge is gone and the position is sold. For weakened positions that are currently losing money, the action is a full sell. For weakened positions that are still profitable, the action is a 50% reduction to lock in partial gains while maintaining exposure in case the thesis recovers.

THRIVING uses an EV-based hold-vs-sell comparison instead of a fixed profit-capture threshold. The bot computes the expected value of holding to resolution (current_model_prob × $1.00 for YES) and compares it to the executable exit value (the current bid with a 2% haircut for spread and slippage). If the executable bid captures at least 95% of the hold EV (EXIT_EV_CAPTURE_RATIO), the bot takes profit. This naturally handles cases where the market is overpaying you to exit — if the bid exceeds your model's hold EV, that's free money and you should take it. It also avoids selling winners too early when the remaining upside (the gap between bid and $1.00) is justified by the model's confidence.

HEALTHY is the default — thesis intact, edge positive, no extreme conditions. The position is held with no action.

A hard stop-loss circuit breaker (EXIT_HARD_STOP_PCT, currently -70%) exists as a last resort for positions where the thesis checks haven't fired but the position is hemorrhaging capital, perhaps due to a model bug or an unusual market structure event. It's intentionally wide so it almost never fires when the thesis-based exits are working correctly. It can be disabled via EXIT_HARD_STOP_ENABLED.

Every exit writes a structured exit_reason to the positions table (e.g., INVALIDATED:observed_max=25.1 > range_high=23.5+0.5), along with an exit_snapshot_id linking to the exact decision context at exit time. The monitor loop now also tracks max_favorable_excursion and max_adverse_excursion on every P&L update, so after a position closes you can see the peak-to-trough range it experienced during its lifetime. Together, these fields let you reconstruct exactly why any exit happened and whether the timing was right.

What we changed about entries
Confidence-weighted Kelly sizing replaces the old binary "half Kelly on normalization warning" rule. The new system computes a smooth confidence multiplier between 0.1 and 1.5, driven by multiple factors. If ECMWF and GFS disagree by more than 2°C (CONFIDENCE_AGREEMENT_THRESHOLD), confidence drops to 0.7×. If the live adjustment is moving μ strongly against the trade direction (sign-aware — a large adjustment toward your bin is confirmatory, not a penalty), confidence drops to 0.7×. Events 3+ days out get a 0.6× discount because forecast reliability degrades with lead time. Normalization warnings still apply a 0.5× penalty, now integrated into the same multiplier. Conversely, if the live adjustment strongly confirms the trade direction, confidence gets a modest 1.1× boost. The multiplier is applied to the raw Kelly fraction, so the final position size is kelly_raw × confidence × time_scale.

Two-regime time scaling addresses your observation that same-day late entries often have the best information quality. Instead of a universal "closer to expiry = smaller position," entries are split into two regimes. Forecast-driven entries (D+1 and beyond) get a gentle discount as expiry approaches — full size for D+2+, 85% for D+1. Observation-driven entries (same-day) use a time-of-day model: morning before 10 AM gets 50% (sparse observations, forecast-like uncertainty), midday 10 AM to 4 PM gets full 100% (the sweet spot where the live adjustment layer has the most data and the model's σ shrinkage is most effective), late afternoon 4-7 PM gets 75% (good data but less execution runway), and evening after 7 PM is blocked by the existing MIN_HOURS_TO_EXPIRY guard.

2-bin bracket entries allow the bot to buy two contiguous bins when they both show edge. After the edge scan identifies primary signals (edge ≥ 7%), a bracket promotion step checks whether an adjacent bin has edge ≥ 4% (BRACKET_MIN_ADJACENT_EDGE). If so, that adjacent bin is added as a signal with Kelly sized at 75% of what independent sizing would produce. The bracket logic is capped at 2 bins per event (BRACKET_MAX_BINS). The value of brackets is that exactly one bin in the group must win (the temperature has to land somewhere), so the combined probability of winning is higher than any individual bin. The bracket naturally scales out via the exit engine: as the day progresses and observations narrow the likely outcome, one bin in the bracket may become INVALIDATED while the other becomes THRIVING.

Portfolio-level risk controls
Total portfolio exposure was lowered from 99% to 15% of bankroll (MAX_TOTAL_EXPOSURE_PCT). Two new concentration caps were added: MAX_EVENT_EXPOSURE_PCT at 3% limits total capital across all bins in a single event, and MAX_CITY_DAY_EXPOSURE at 5% limits total capital for any one city on a given date. These gates run as part of the existing risk check sequence before any order is placed. The intent is that before enabling brackets (which naturally increase per-event exposure), the portfolio risk controls are already tight enough to prevent overconcentration.

The new .env variables and what to tune
The exit thresholds — EXIT_DEAD_BUFFER_C, EXIT_DYING_PROB_THRESHOLD, EXIT_WEAKENED_SIGMA_SHIFT, EXIT_EV_CAPTURE_RATIO, and EXIT_HARD_STOP_PCT — are all set to conservative defaults. After running in paper mode for a week, the exit_reason field on closed positions will tell you which classifications are firing and whether they're catching real problems or being too aggressive. If INVALIDATED exits are happening on positions that would have recovered, widen the buffer. If DYING exits are firing on positions that later rebounded, raise the probability threshold. The positions.max_favorable_excursion and max_adverse_excursion fields will show you the counterfactual — "if I hadn't exited here, how much better or worse would it have gotten?"
